## Summary

On January 26, 2026, Umi and Mathew met through FaceTime to discuss ideas for our recruitment materials. We also made a comprehensive, but not yet exhaustive list of inclusion and exclusion criteria. By the end of the meeting, we made rough drafts of a flyer and an email. In the future, we plan on finalizing our rough drafts and presenting them to our recruitment advisor, Mugisha Niyibizi from Georgia CTSA's recruitment center, on January 29th at 2:30 PM.

---

## Background

As novice undergraduate researchers, it has not occurred to us that we need a comprehensive recruitment plan before we can submit the IRB proposal. While we believed this to be a relatively simple step in our design (considering all other things), we decided to allocate at least one week to synthesize adequate recruitment materials and a written plan of preparation and dissemination.

---

## Eligibility Criteria

On January 26, 2026 at 7:00 PM, we held a virtual meeting brainstorming possible ideas for our recruitment items. We concluded that two mechanisms of recruitment, a passive flyer and an email, may be best for maximizing the number of responses we receive while staying within our eligibility criteria.

### Inclusion Criteria

While both the eligibility criteria and the mechanisms of recruitment are subject to change, our inclusion criteria are as follows:
{{< details summary="Inclusion Criteria" >}}
* Undergraduate students currently enrolled at Emory University
* Students that are at least 18 years of age and, thus, can independently consent to participate
* All genders, races and ethnic groups (inclusive of women and minorities)
* Those who are able to take about 30 minutes out of their day to complete our one-time survey
{{< /details >}}

### Exclusion Criteria

Conservely, our exclusion criteria are as follows:
{{< details summary="Exclusion Criteria" >}}
* Adults unable to consent
* Alumni that have graduated from Emory University or any other academic institutions
* Adults that are currently employed as faculty at Emory University or any other academic institutions, including formally appointed personnel responsible for instruction, research, and service. These encompass ranks, including but not limited to, professor, associate professor, assistant professor, and instructor on both tenure and non-tenure tracks
* Students not currently attending Emory University
* Students currently attending Emory University that are enrolled to obtain a Master's Degree (MA, MS, MBA, MFA, MEd), a Doctoral or Professional Degree (PhD, MD/DO, JD, PharmD, DDS/DMD, DVM, PsyD, EdD), and a Licentiate Degree (if applicable)
* Prisoners
* Students who are vulnerable to coercion or undue influence
* Students under the age of 18 and, thus, require consent from a parent or guardian
* <mark>Cognitively impaired or individuals with impaired decision-making capacity</mark> (This is currently up for debate)
{{< /details >}}

These lists are not exhaustive, and we're not exactly sure whether they should be. We are also not sure about whether to exclude cognitively impaired students, since this can encompass a broad range of students, from those diagnosed with dyslexia to ADHD. We may remove this item from the exclusion criteria, as this may lead to our data being less representative. These are uncertainties that we will discuss further with Dr. Maxim as we finish the recruitment materials.

## Design of Recruitment Materials

As mentioned previously, our two mechanisms of recruitment are disseminating passive flyers and an email. We hope that, with cooperation from Dr. Maxim, the email will be distributed to the listserv of the Modern Languages department.

For both formats, we referenced Emory IRB's general guidelines for advertising and recruitment materials, which states that they *must* include the following:

{{< details summary="Recruitment Guidelines">}}
*	The name and address of the researcher and/or research facility; 
*	The condition under study (if applicable) and/or the purpose of the research; 
*	In summary form, the criteria that will be used to determine eligibility for the study; 
*	A brief list of participation benefits, if any (e.g., a no-cost health examination, participation in a nutrition program, etc…); 
*	The time or other commitment required of the subjects; and 
*	The location of the research and the person or office to contact for further information.

<mark>**Important**: compensation must not be highlighted/accentuated beyond the other information about the study.</mark>
{{< /details>}}

### Email Design (Credit: Mathew Kang)


Dear [intended participant/s],

We are emailing you to invite you to be part of our research project about students’ attitudes on academic close reading, language’s role as a device for learning, and academic AI use.

Our goal of this new study is to see how linguistically indifferent American college students are. In other words, how closely do you pay attention when reading something handed to you in class or just in everyday life? 

While you will not receive compensation for the study, our hope is that, through your responses, we can uncover gaps in the way that professors teach core literary skills. Your participation will also help us make a standardized scale that will measure students’ ability to maintain close attention to language, with the goal of improving the quality of education in the future university.
